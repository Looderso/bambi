CUES

MATCH

The KU100 split as in the thesis: what the ears share (CLL) and how they differ (ILD, ITD). Head size scales the time between the ears and stretches the level difference in frequency — a larger head shadows from lower up. Ear size stretches the shared part's direction cues — a larger ear puts the notches lower. The timbre, the shared part's average over all directions, stays the KU100's.

LISTEN FOR

Turn the head slowly, left and right of the loudspeaker: the virtual one should stay on it. If it swims — moves with the head, or drifts the other way — head size is off.

Tilt the head down and up, so the loudspeaker is above or below you: the virtual one should rise and fall with it. If it stays level, or flips front to back, try ear size.

Match where, not what: the real loudspeaker has a room around it, the virtual one has none.
